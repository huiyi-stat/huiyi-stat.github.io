Welcome! I am a statistician working in population research, from general populations to studies of sensitive, hidden, and hard-to-reach populations, I develop methodological frameworks for social and health challenges: machine learning, deep learning, Hierarchical Bayesian Models, the network scale-up and respondent-driven sampling methods, tailored to national and regional contexts. These frameworks have produced baseline and endline prevalence estimates of the sensitive phenomena that informed intervention programs for the communities most in need.

My intervention and implementation research designs programs for populations, such as adults at risk of dementia, women who have experienced interpersonal violence, and adolescent girls affected by unhealthy relationships, and uses experimental designs (such as pre- and post-design, randomized controlled trials) to evaluate outcomes and cost-effectiveness. With domestic and international partners, I build ethics-centered, community-participatory data collection: training fieldwork teams in mapping, logistics, rigorous sampling, rapport, and care-centered interviewing, and then analyzing data, writing reports and articles, and presenting findings together.

I teach statistics and statistical deep learning to undergraduate and graduate students each year. I serve as Associate Editor of Statistics and Public Policy, Consulting Editor of Teaching Educational Research Methods, and a reviewer for journals including The Annals of Applied Statistics and the Journal of Human Trafficking.
