Welcome! I am a statistician who develops statistical and machine learning methods for measuring hidden and hard-to-reach populations. Much of my work estimates how common child, sex, and labor trafficking are in places where conventional surveys fall short, using respondent-driven and link-tracing sampling, the network scale-up method, small area estimation, and Bayesian modeling. With colleagues at the University of Georgia, I have applied these methods in Sierra Leone, Guinea, Senegal, Malawi, and Zambia.

I am now building deep learning and graph-based approaches to social network analysis for data-deficient settings, and I serve as Co-PI on an NSF project bringing responsible generative AI into undergraduate statistics and data science courses.
